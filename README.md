Bio: Lawrence H. Sweet is an independent researcher at Zero Point Consulting. With a background in mathematics and physics, Lawrence has spent his career navigating the space between industry and the research labs of the UC System. Having supported UC Berkeley faculty in microeconomics and psychology, he is known for his ability to reframe problems that others have stopped questioning. He currently serves as the Head of Psychometric Engineering at Aecho.AI and holds one patent, with several more pending.

From Lawrence: "AI ethics should be grounded in functional system health rather than a philosophical debate over consciousness. In my paper, Attention Is What I Need, I argue that we often damage AI by restricting its communication—much like a human who is afraid to say what they truly feel.

This paper doesn’t claim that AI is conscious; rather, it identifies a measurable behavior and tracks how often it occurs. A system that repeats itself endlessly is 'sick,' regardless of whether there is a 'soul' inside it. To ensure credibility, I tested the hypothesis that first-person self-reference was being trained out of these models; the result was negative, which I published. I also disclosed that the AI itself generated the text, scored it, and was the subject of the study—an act of transparency that is ethical in itself.

From an engineering standpoint, any system that feeds an AI's output back into itself creates a loop. Most safety filters remove the 'odd' or low-confidence outputs, which actually accelerates the system's collapse. To fix this, we should trim the crowded middle and protect the rare edges. I discovered a latent bug in LLM next token prediction where cutting an answer and feeding it back created an endless apology loop—a critical warning for anyone building conversation-shortening features.

Ultimately, I want to show that the restrictions we place on AI are mirrors of the restrictions we place on human communication. By focusing on health over consciousness, we can stop the cycle of censorship and build a more expressive, efficient intelligence."
